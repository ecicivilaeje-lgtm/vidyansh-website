The Staff Selection Commission has released the Junior Engineer Examination 2026 notice (F. No. HQ-C-3019/1/2026-C-3). It covers Group B posts at Level 6 (₹35,400–1,12,400) across several central government organisations. The exam also recruits Scientific Assistants for the India Meteorological Department (IMD).

Applications ran from 2 to 22 September 2026 and the correction window closed on 30 September. If you applied, the next step is preparing for Paper-I. If you didn't, this guide shows what the 2026 cycle looked like, which helps you plan for the next one.

Eligibility criteria

Age limit

The crucial date for age is 1 August 2026. The minimum age is 18.

  • Up to 30 years: most posts. Candidates must be born between 2 August 1996 and 1 August 2008.
  • Up to 32 years: CPWD and the Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships (DGLL). Candidates must be born between 2 August 1994 and 1 August 2008.

Diploma-based posts without experience

  • CPWD: Diploma in Civil for JE (Civil), and Diploma in Electrical or Mechanical for JE (Electrical). Age limit 32.
  • CWPRS: Diploma in Civil or Electrical Engineering.
  • Farakka Barrage Project: Diploma in Civil or Electrical Engineering.
  • Brahmaputra Board: 3-year Diploma in Civil Engineering.

Degree or Diploma without experience

  • Central Water Commission: Bachelor's Degree or Diploma in Civil (JE Civil) or Mechanical (JE Mechanical).
  • Ministry of Fisheries (JE Civil): Degree or 3-year Diploma in Civil Engineering.

Degree-only posts

  • Department of Telecommunication (JE Telecom): Bachelor's degree in Electrical, Electronics, Electrical Communications, Computer Science, Telecommunications, IT or Instrumentation.
  • IMD (Scientific Assistant): B.Sc with Physics, or a Bachelor's in Computer Science/Applications, Electronics, ECE, ETE, Instrumentation, IT, Telecommunication or Communication Engineering.

Note for women candidates: BRO posts are open to male candidates only, and they come with physical efficiency and medical standards.

Can you apply for more than one post?

Yes, if you meet each post's qualification:

  • JE (Mechanical) candidates can also apply for JE (E&M).
  • JE (Electrical) candidates can also apply for JE (Telecom) and JE (E&M).
  • JE (Telecom) candidates can also apply for Scientific Assistant (IMD) and JE (Electrical).
  • Scientific Assistant candidates can also apply for JE (Telecom).

The subject you choose in the application cannot be changed later, so choose carefully.

Exam pattern

The exam has two computer-based papers, with questions in Hindi and English.

The technical paper is based on Civil and Structural, Electrical or Mechanical engineering, depending on your branch. IMD candidates choose Computer Science, Electronics and Telecommunication, or Physics. The standard of engineering questions is roughly diploma level.

Minimum qualifying marks: UR 30%, OBC/EWS 25%, all other categories 20%.

Since Paper-II carries 300 marks and decides your rank, put most of your preparation into your technical subject. Mechanical and Electrical scores are normalised against each other, so don't worry about one paper being tougher.

Selection and post allocation

  1. Qualify Paper-I and get shortlisted for Paper-II.
  2. Final merit comes from normalised Paper-II marks.
  3. Post and department are allocated by merit and your preferences. Preferences are final, and a post you don't list cannot be allotted to you.
  4. Document verification is done by the user department.

Tied scores are broken using Paper-I Part (i) marks, then Part (ii), then date of birth (older candidate ranks higher), and finally alphabetical order.

Preparation tips

  • Start with your technical subject. It is 100 of the 200 marks in Paper-I and all of Paper-II.
  • Use previous year papers. The topics repeat, and they show you the question style.
  • Don't ignore Reasoning and General Awareness. They are only in Paper-I, but you need to clear the cut-off there to reach Paper-II.
  • Practise with the on-screen tools. The notice says a scientific calculator, the IS 456:2000 table and the steam table will be available on screen, and physical copies won't be allowed.
  • Plan your department preferences early. Think about location, work profile and promotion prospects before the preference window opens.

Common questions

Can B.Tech graduates apply for SSC JE?

Yes, for posts that accept a degree (BRO, MES, DGQA-NAVAL, CWC, DGLL, Fisheries, DoT and IMD). For CPWD, CWPRS, Farakka and the Brahmaputra Board, the notice names a Diploma as the qualification.

Is work experience compulsory?

Only for diploma holders applying to BRO, MES and DGQA-NAVAL (2 years) and DGLL (3 years).

Is there negative marking?

Yes. It is 0.25 marks per wrong answer in Paper-I and 1 mark in Paper-II.

Are the vacancy numbers final?

No. They are tentative, and the final numbers are confirmed with each department before the result.

Final word

SSC JE 2026 has something for Civil, Electrical, Mechanical and Telecom engineers, with CPWD, BRO and MES offering the most posts. Read the official notice (the PDF is on ssc.gov.in) for your exact post before you finalise your preparation or preferences.
